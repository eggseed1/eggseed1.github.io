first ramblings // october?

oct 24, 2026

i haven't blogged or written much about my thoughts in a while (especially not in english...) so this feels a little awkward. i've been staring at the stupid blinking line on an empty google doc for longer than i'd like to admit, trying to figure out what exactly i'm supposed to write about, or if i even have anything to say; which is funny because i spend so much of my day thinking about things that i'd like to write about, and then when i finally sit down to do it all those thoughts suddenly become very uninteresting. but i think there's some value in forcing myself through this initial discomfort, since i've recently come to realize that i feel the most motivated about my life when i'm actively writing, as though i need to have some written record of my thoughts to convince myself that i've actually been thinking them, and that my days haven't just passed through me without leaving much of anything behind.

first thing on my mind is that i've felt like i've lost a little part of myself every time i've entered a new phase of my life. when i first went off to college i had this strange desire to finally fit in, to start over in a place where nobody knew me and where i could become some new and improved version of myself without having to answer for whoever i was before. and i succeeded, for a bit! though somewhere in the process i also lost much of the passion i had for the things i used to love. some were inevitable, like being unable to keep pet frogs or plants in a dorm, but a lot of it was entirely my own doing; i stopped drawing, stopped reading nearly as much, stopped doing all these little things that had occupied so much of my time before college, and for no particular reason either. i just stopped. i suppose i was so preoccupied with becoming someone else that i didn't really care to preserve much of the person i already was. it took me quite some time to find my way back to those interests, and to discover new ones, and to feel that little spark of being completely taken with something again.

and now, having graduated college and entered the beautiful world of post-grad corporate slop, i find myself going through the whole thing all over again, except this time there's no obvious new person to become, and i'm not even entirely sure what it is that i've lost. i keep trying to get myself interested in things, you see; i bought a sewing machine recently because i've been wanting to make my own clothes, to recreate vintage styles and have some actual freedom in the way i dress instead of just mindlessly buying more and more clothes (though i am still doing that too, unfortunately, i have redownloaded depop 8 time this month). and i do want to learn! i've wanted to for years! but there's this strange feeling that i'm having to drag myself into enjoying these things, that i'm trying to will some great passion into existence rather than allowing it to find me on its own. I feel like i’m going through the motions of being someone who has hobbies, and hoping that eventually the motions will produce the feeling.

i wish i could have that old excitement again, the kind that almost makes you sick with how much you want to know about something. i remember hearing cecil taylor's air for the first time and feeling like something had been pried open inside my head, like there was this entire world of music that had been existing perfectly well without me knowing anything about it, and now that i knew it was there i couldn't bear not knowing more; or the first time i heard blind willie mctell's east st. louis blues, which sent me into this months-long frenzy of listening and reading and looking for anything even remotely related to it. i was so infatuated! i wanted to hear everything, know everything, dig my head so deep into it that there was no room left for anything else. or, more embarrassingly, when lost ark first came out while i was in high school and i became completely obsessed with it, to the point where i don't think there was a single waking hour when i wasn't thinking about the stupid game. and i know this isn't a particularly healthy way of enjoying things, that i probably shouldn't need some all-consuming obsession to feel motivated about my own life, but there's nothing quite like waking up already excited about something. having a thing that makes the hours feel too short instead of too long. without it i feel a little aimless, and everything becomes this vapid succession of things to do and things to consume, none of which seem to matter all that much once they're over.

though maybe this is just what getting older is like..? i don't know. i've been learning to fingerpick guitar again, slowly but surely, and it really is a wonderful feeling to hear the progress i've made from the week before, even if it's something as little as finally being able to play a passage without my fingers getting tangled up. there's something particularly nice about learning to play the old country and blues standards i've loved for years, and finding that my admiration for the musicians only grows the more i struggle to reproduce what they made sound so effortless. i feel a little closer to them, somehow, even if the distance between my playing and theirs is still rather enormous. and it's nice to actually make something again instead of just listening to someone else do it, even if what i'm making is a rather bad imitation. perhaps i've gotten so accustomed to the instant gratification of discovering and consuming things that i've forgotten how to enjoy the slow, rather boring process of getting better at something. which is a shame, because i do enjoy it once i actually sit down and start. it's the sitting down that's difficult.

and then there's work on the top of my mind... which i feel almost embarrassed complaining about because i know how ridiculously privileged my situation is. i work remotely, get paid well, barely work five hours a day, and have enough free time to indulge in all these things i'm constantly complaining about not having the motivation to do. so really what is there to complain about … and yet i find it so mentally exhausting, not even because the work itself is particularly difficult, but because there's something deeply dispiriting about spending so much of my life doing something i don't care about, and knowing that i'll probably continue doing it for the foreseeable future because i've grown rather fond of being paid. i have this awful inferiority complex toward people who can simply give up their corporate jobs to do something more meaningful, who can make a living studying or writing or working in some field they genuinely believe in. i wish i could throw everything behind and spend the rest of my days studying the things i love, but then i think about what i'd actually have to give up to do that and suddenly the idea becomes a lot less romantic. i want the freedom without the sacrifice, the satisfaction without the uncertainty, and preferably all of it while maintaining my current salary. very unrealistic demands i think.

i saw this ragebait tiktok recently of a girl wearing this beautiful 70s gunne sax three-piece outfit, with a caption along the lines of "no one knows i'm wearing 70s gunne sax at my sf ai startup" and the comments were absolutely flaming her, saying she didn't deserve to dress vintage while working for an ai company, that she was somehow betraying the culture or ideals associated with the clothes she was wearing. which i found a little ridiculous because i don't think you need to pass some moral examination to be allowed to wear an old dress, but it also gave me this horrible pang of guilt, because here i am working in crypto while being so deeply enamored with vintage fashion, music, and culture, spending my free time reading all these critiques of capitalism and consumerism and then going right back to participating in the very things i claim to dislike. and it's not even as though i'm unaware of the contradiction. i'm painfully aware of it, i just don't really do anything about it.

i keep telling myself that i don't have the financial privilege to pursue a less lucrative but more ethical or fulfilling career, which is a very convenient thing to believe because it means i never actually have to make the decision; but really, would i be starving if i took a twenty or thirty percent pay cut? probably not. i'd be less comfortable, certainly, and would be a bit more anxious about my future, but i'd be fine. so perhaps i ought to stop pretending that my circumstances are entirely outside of my control and admit that money means quite a lot to me, more than i'd like it to, and that i'm perfectly willing to let my brain rot away for the sake of a comfortable life. the golden handcuffs are very soft and pleasant, actually; i keep talking about wanting to escape them while making sure they're fastened nice and snug around my wrists.

and what is the value of reading all these theories if i'm just going to keep them tucked away somewhere in the back of my mind, bringing them out whenever i want to feel intellectually or morally superior to something, then promptly putting them away when they become inconvenient? it all feels like some elaborate performative fuckery, a way to convince myself that knowing what's wrong with the world is somehow equivalent to doing something about it. but then i think, well, what are the alternatives? what am i supposed to do? quit my job and go live in the woods? which is probably another convenient little excuse, because i've managed to turn the possibility of changing anything into the absurdity of having to change everything, and naturally the latter sounds impossible so i get to keep doing nothing. very clever of me.

but whatever, ai is going to doom us all anyways. accelerate accelerate accelerate until everything is reduced to one enormous pile of generated slop and nobody can remember what people used to do with their time. at least my childhood was safe. yay!!

so instead of doing anything particularly meaningful about any of this i've been spending an embarrassing amount of time doomscrolling, actually feeling my brain deteriorate as i do it.. but when im not doing that i've been consuming a lot of westerns lately, which have become something of a forced fixation, though i suppose i've always been a little infatuated with that world. there's something about the landscapes and clothes and music and the idiotic masculinity of it all that i find impossibly attractive. i genuinely wake up some days wishing i were a small white twink with a flat, thin, frail little body, wearing tiny distressed true vintage clothes and looking like some half-starved cowboy who's been sleeping in a barn for three weeks. i want the narrow shoulders, the sunken cheeks, the stetson hat, the salt and peppers pants that feel oversized on my body. very specific aspirations. and i can't help feeling that these things look a little odd on my own asian female body, that there's something about the image i have in my head that i can't quite reproduce no matter how much i try. which you could probably attribute to some internalized sexism or racism or whatever, and perhaps you'd be right, but i'm not really interested in pretending that i don't feel that way just because there's a more socially acceptable way i ought to feel about it. i want to look like a cowboy. unfortunately god had other plans.

but beyond the clothes and the beautiful men and the whole ridiculous macho posturing of it all, i think what i really love about westerns are the ramblers. the drifters, the wandering cowboys, these men who seem to exist just a little outside of the world everyone else has agreed to live in, who come into town with nothing much to their name, get themselves tangled up in some trouble or another, and then leave again as though there was never any reason to stay. i love the thought of a person whose whole life can be packed onto the back of a horse, who doesn't need to belong anywhere or be anybody in particular, whose past can remain his own and whose future is just some indistinct place over the next hill. and i know that the reality of that sort of life is probably miserable, that there's nothing particularly romantic about being poor and lonely and having nowhere to go, but there's something so beautiful about that lack of attachment, or at least the appearance of it, that makes my own life feel terribly small and crowded in comparison.

and perhaps that's precisely why i'm so attracted to these ramblers, because i'm far too much of a coward to ever become one. i want so badly to escape the little system i've built around myself, or that i've allowed to be built around me, but i also want all the comforts and securities that come with being trapped inside it. i want to be free to wander wherever i please, except i'd also like to know that i'll have a warm bed to return to, and money in my bank account, and all the people i love. i want the freedom of having nothing while being unwilling to give up anything. and i can't help but find it a little pathetic, this enormous admiration i have for people who can simply leave everything behind when i can't even bring myself to leave a job i don't particularly like (this is also a lie). the rambler moves through the world with so little to hold onto, while i seem to spend all my time acquiring more and more things to tether myself down with; and the more i acquire, the more frightened i become of losing them.

though i suppose the ramblers i admire are hardly free in the way i imagine them to be. half of them are running away from something, or have been driven out of somewhere, or simply have no place left where anybody wants them; and perhaps what i take for freedom is just another kind of imprisonment, the sort where you're condemned to keep moving because there's nowhere you can bear to stay. but even that has a certain appeal to me, which probably says more about my own romantic stupidity than anything else. there's something wonderfully attractive about a person who has been stripped of all these ordinary attachments and has nothing left to do but go on living, even if the actual experience of being that person would be awful. i'm quite happy to admire their suffering from the comfort of my room

but i think what i envy most is not necessarily the wandering itself, but the possibility of being able to wander, of being the sort of person who can leave without feeling as though they're tearing themselves away from everything that makes them who they are. i've always had this desire to become somebody else, from wanting to reinvent myself in college to wanting to dress like some obscure figure out of a hundred-year-old photograph, and perhaps all of these attempts at finding myself have really just been ways of avoiding the rather boring business of being myself. i keep searching for some ideal life in other people, other places, other periods of history, hoping that if i can arrange all the little pieces correctly i'll eventually feel that wonderful certainty of being exactly who i'm supposed to be. but there's always something missing, and then i find another thing to be infatuated with and begin the whole process again.

i don't think the affection itself is false. i really do love these things, and i love learning about them, and sometimes i feel a little silly worrying so much about whether my enjoyment of them is authentic enough when the enjoyment is one of the few things i feel completely certain about. perhaps there's no great moral failing in wanting to play pretend for a while. i can put on my ridiculous little cowboy clothes, listen to marty robbins, and spend an afternoon imagining myself somewhere far away from all the things i dislike about my life, and then return to those very things the next morning, still dressed in pajamas and answering slack messages. i haven't actually escaped anything, but for a little while i get to feel as though i have. maybe that's all i can reasonably ask of it.

anyways, life's not too bad. i have my stupid comfortable job, a sewing machine i really ought to use more often, a guitar that i'm slowly getting better at playing, and what feels like an endless supply of books and records and films to occupy the rest of my life. i do wonder sometimes whether i'm really living through all these things or just finding more and more elaborate ways to pass the time until something happens to me, but that's a rather unpleasant thought and i'd prefer not to dwell on it too much. larp is the only way out!!
