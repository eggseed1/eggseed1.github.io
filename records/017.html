original collection notes

A lot of folks know J.J. Cale as the great legend behind the Tulsa Sound, with a hit career that stretched from the late 1950s all the way to his posthumous releases in 2019. But not many realize his very first appearance on record came way back in 1958, when he was still going by Johnny Cale. That year, he played lead guitar on a rare 45 rpm single featuring two tracks by Al Sweatt: “I Hate Myself” and “Paint the Town Red.”

This single comes with a quite a hefty price tag, not just because of its rarity, but because it marks the start of Cale’s recording career. On “I Hate Myself,” the guitar riff towards the end is especially memorable. It’s got a sharp swing to it: punchy, clean, and already showing signs of the cool, understated style Cale would later be known for. The B-side, “Paint the Town Red,” is a rougher, more driving rockabilly number that’s full of raw energy.

While Cale went on to shape an entire sound and influence artists like Clapton and Knopfler, Al Sweatt didn’t stick around the music scene for long. After this single, he took more than a decade off to focus on his family and only made a short-lived return in the ‘70s. It’s a bit of a shame as both tracks are fantastic, and his songwriting had real spark... 😞
